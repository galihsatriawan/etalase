## How to use this page

This page is the Academy version of `docs/15-evidence.md`, the master evidence file. Every primary link below was re-opened on **10 October 2026** for this page; the log is at the bottom. Where a source said something slightly different from `docs/15`, the card says so.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| <Badge type="tip" text="Verified" /> | The source says it, in the words we use. Say it plainly. |
| <Badge type="warning" text="Nuance" /> | True, but only with the qualifier on the card (year, scope, secondary source). Always say the qualifier. |
| <Badge type="danger" text="Not supported" /> | No source backs it. It has been removed from the docs and deck; never say it. |

Quotes are copied from the source (short excerpts). "Data year" is the period the number describes, which is often older than the publication date.

::: warning If it is not on this page, do not say it
In Q&A, open this page, find the card, read the quote, then give the "If a judge asks" answer. Do not improvise a number.
:::

## Social and chat commerce

### 4. 95% of Indonesian online businesses sell through chat apps; 18% use marketplaces <Badge type="warning" text="Nuance" />

> "The majority, or 95.33%, of Indonesian e-commerce businesses sell through instant messaging applications"
>
> "Furthermore, 33.29% sell on social media, and only 17.80% utilize marketplaces." "...1.77% through websites."

- **Where:** deck slide 2, `docs/01`.
- **Data year:** 2023 (BPS *E-Commerce Statistics 2023*, catalog 8101004, published 30 Jan 2025). Survey of "40,011 e-commerce businesses spread across 38 provinces and 365 regencies/cities".
- **Source:** primary [BPS publication page](https://www.bps.go.id/en/publication/2025/01/30/d52af11843aee401403ecfa6/statistik-e-commerce-2023.html) (the page shows only the abstract; the channel table is in the PDF, which we could not open) · quoted by [Databoks, 7 Feb 2025](https://databoks.katadata.co.id/en/technology-telecommunications/statistics/67a5866310206/few-local-e-commerce-players-sell-on-marketplaces) and [GoodStats, 28 Feb 2025](https://goodstats.id/article/mayoritas-usaha-e-commerce-berjualan-lewat-aplikasi-pesan-instan-HmFph)
- **Nuance:** businesses use several channels (shares add up to 151.5%), so it is "95% *use* chat", not "95% sell *only* through chat". The base is any business that took orders over the internet in 2023, so a food stall on WhatsApp counts: the figure is a floor effect of that definition and shows that chat is the default inbox, **not** that 95% of sellers lack a store system. BPS gives no overlap between chat and marketplace sellers, and nobody has measured chat-first product sellers. The 2024 edition (published 28 Nov 2025, about 4.4 million businesses) exists and its channel table is unread. The BPS table number is still an open item in `docs/15`: download the PDF before the pitch and note it.
- **If a judge asks** "Is this official?": "Yes, it is Statistics Indonesia's 2023 e-commerce survey of about 40,000 businesses. 95% use instant messaging to sell; only 18% use marketplaces. Businesses can use several channels, and the survey counts any business that took orders online, so we read it as chat being the default order inbox. We have not measured the share of chat-only sellers; that is what our seller interviews are for."

### 5. About 1 in 2 Southeast Asian shoppers have bought or sold on social or messaging apps <Badge type="warning" text="Nuance" />

> "Close to half of the shoppers said they have bought or sold on social media or messaging platforms"

- **Where:** deck slide 2 ("2019 survey"), `docs/01`.
- **Data year:** 2019 (Facebook × BCG study, fieldwork May to August 2019, 5,391 respondents in Southeast Asia).
- **Source:** the original report was not found online; press coverage: [KrASIA, 1 Nov 2019](https://kr-asia.com/shoppers-in-southeast-asia-prefer-to-buy-using-chat-or-voice-applications-according-to-joint-study) · [e27, Oct 2019](https://e27.co/southeast-asia-emerges-as-leader-in-conversational-commerce-thailand-vietnam-most-advanced-in-adoption-20191031/) ("Approximately 47 per cent said they conduct conversational commerce on social media or messaging platforms")
- **Nuance:** Thailand over 40%, Vietnam 36%, **Indonesia 29%**, US 5% (e27 and KrASIA). Never say "half of Indonesians". The study was funded by Facebook. e27 gives 47% social media or chat, 44% e-commerce platforms and 9% brand sites (8,864 respondents), which suggests a split of channels rather than "47% of all shoppers" (our inference); KrASIA's wording is "close to half". Prefer to drop this card for Indonesia.
- **If a judge asks** "Isn't 2019 data old?": "Yes. It is the only cross-country Southeast Asia comparison we found, so we use it only for the regional picture. For Indonesia we use BPS 2023, which is newer and official."

### 6. Discovery on TikTok or Instagram usually ends in a WhatsApp chat <Badge type="warning" text="Nuance" />

- **Where:** `docs/01`. **Quote:** none; this is an observation, not a statistic.
- **Closest data:** BPS 2023 via Databoks: 95.33% use instant messaging and 33.29% social media (claim 4).
- **If a judge asks** "Where is that from?": "It is what we see in seller conversations, supported indirectly by BPS: almost every online business uses chat, a third also use social media. We will measure it in the pilot."

### 7. Sellers keep catalogs in photos, captions, voice notes and spreadsheets <Badge type="warning" text="Nuance" />

- **Where:** `docs/01`, deck. **Quote:** none (product insight).
- **Closest data:** only 1.77% of Indonesian e-commerce businesses sell through a website (Databoks, BPS 2023), so very few have a structured store catalog.
- **If a judge asks** "How do you know?": "Only about 2% sell through their own website, so most have no product database. The photo-and-voice-note pattern is our observation; seller interviews in the pilot will quantify it."

### 8. "About 80% of online merchants sell through social commerce" <Badge type="danger" text="Not supported" />

> "80% of online merchants in Indonesia are selling through social commerce" — attributed to "press statements by Orderfaz and 1982 Ventures" ([Digital News Asia, 13 Jul 2023](https://digitalnewsasia.com/startups/indonesian-fintech-orderfaz-raises-pre-seed-round-1982-ventures))

> "Just 11% of small- to medium-sized businesses (SMBs) qualify as agent-ready, compared with 19% of large merchants."

- **Where:** deck slide 2, `docs/01`, README.
- **Data year:** 2026 (PYMNTS Intelligence × Visa Acceptance Solutions, *2026 Global Digital Shopping Index: Merchant Edition*, article 22 Sep 2026).

- **If a judge asks** "Does that apply to Indonesia?": "It is a global survey. We have no Indonesian figure. A seller whose catalog lives in photos and chats probably has no structured product data, so we expect them to be at or below the SMB average, but that is an inference."

### 10. 15% of merchants have structured product data <Badge type="tip" text="Verified" />

> "15% of merchants overall have structured product data for agent access."

- **Data year:** 2026 (same index). **Source:** [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/smbs/2026/visa-says-small-merchants-could-move-faster-on-ai-shopping/) · [Forkast, 23 Sep 2026](https://forkast.news/?p=130650) ("only 15% of merchants possess the structured product data necessary for agents")
- **Nuance:** "merchants overall", not SMBs only.
- **If a judge asks** "15% of small merchants?": "No, 15% of all merchants in the survey. That is exactly the gap Etalase fills: it creates the structured data."

### 11. "Big retailers and platform merchants are ready by default" <Badge type="danger" text="Not supported" />

- **Where:** deck, "Why now" paragraph. **Quote:** none.
- **Problem:** the same PYMNTS index says only 19% of large merchants are agent-ready (claim 9), which contradicts "ready by default".
- **Safer wording:** "Platforms such as Shopify and the marketplaces are bringing their own merchants to AI channels; independent chat sellers have no such route." (claims 44 and 47).
- **If a judge asks** "Are big retailers really ready?": "Not all: PYMNTS finds 19% of large merchants agent-ready. Our point is that platforms are building agent channels for their own merchants, and chat sellers are outside every platform."

### 13. UCP works over REST, A2A and MCP, with AP2 for payments, and a profile at /.well-known/ucp <Badge type="tip" text="Verified" />

> "It also provides businesses flexible ways to integrate via APIs, Agent2Agent (A2A), and the Model Context Protocol (MCP)."
>
> "is compatible with Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) to provide secure agentic payments support."
>
> "a standard JSON manifest located at /.well-known/ucp"

- **Data year:** 2026. **Source:** [Google Developers Blog, 11 Jan 2026](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/under-the-hood-universal-commerce-protocol-ucp/) · [UCP specification overview](https://ucp.dev/2026-04-08/specification/overview/) (lists REST via OpenAPI, MCP via OpenRPC, A2A via Agent Card, and "supports the AP2 Mandates Extension")
- **Honesty note:** Etalase implements the UCP Checkout, Fulfillment and Order capabilities and the buyer-consent extension over REST (spec 2026-04-08) and runs Google's conformance suite against them: 31 of 77 tests pass in production mode and 54 in a sandbox test mode that lets the suite pay with its mock card, and each failure is explained ([UCP checkout](/architecture/ucp)). There is no UCP certification, nothing has been tried with Google's own surfaces, and AP2 is roadmap.
- **If a judge asks** "Are you UCP compliant?": "We implement UCP's checkout and fulfillment over REST and run Google's own conformance suite against it. It passes discovery, idempotency, totals and the fulfillment flow, and fails 22 tests that assume card payment, order or discount capabilities we do not offer, or the sample server's test hooks. Every failure is listed with its reason. We don't claim certification, and we have not tried it with AI Mode."

## How to cite in the pitch

1. **Number, source, year, in one breath.** "95% of Indonesian e-commerce businesses sell through chat apps, according to Statistics Indonesia's 2023 survey."
2. **Say the qualifier the card asks for:** "projected" (claim 1), "US only" (claim 21), "2019" (claim 5), "testing in India" (claim 17), "subsidised campaign" (claim 20).
3. **Prefer the primary source** in the deck footer (BPS, Bain, PYMNTS × Visa, Morgan Stanley), and keep secondary links for Q&A.
4. **Do not round up.** 95.33% becomes "95%", 17.80% "18%"; never "almost all" or "only 1 in 5".
5. **Separate facts from estimates.** Market figures are sourced; cost figures are "our estimates from list prices" (`docs/10`); benchmark results marked TBD in the deck are not results yet.
6. **Have this page open on your phone** during Q&A and the claim number ready.

## Check yourself

1. A judge says "95% of Indonesians shop on WhatsApp". What is wrong, and what is the correct statement?
::: details Answer
The BPS figure is about businesses, not shoppers, and about using chat, not using it exclusively. Correct: "95% of Indonesian e-commerce businesses sell through instant messaging apps (BPS, 2023 data); businesses can use several channels."
:::

2. Which three words must you add when quoting the Morgan Stanley forecast?
::: details Answer
"US", "by 2030" and "forecast" (a range of US$190–385B, about 10% likely, up to 20% optimistic).
:::

3. Is UCP checkout live in India? In Indonesia?
::: details Answer
India: a limited Flipkart test in Gemini since late September 2026, possibly not on Google-hosted UCP checkout. Indonesia: named among the next markets, no date.
:::

4. Why is "QRIS costs 0.7%" misleading for Etalase sellers?
::: details Answer
Since 1 October 2026 the MDR is 0% for micro merchants up to Rp500,000 per transaction (and for everyone up to Rp100,000). The real payment cost risk is the gateway's fixed fee, IDR 4,000 on Xendit's list.
:::

::: details Answer
Any two of: "80% of merchants sell through social commerce", "only a few percent of transactions involve agents", the learnwithhasan QRIS fee link, "Shopify merchants only", "T+7 settlement" and, as worded, "big retailers are ready by default".
:::
